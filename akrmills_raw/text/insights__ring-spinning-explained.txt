# Ring Spinning Explained: Why It Remains the Most Trusted Yarn Manufacturing Method
Ring spinning has been the backbone of cotton yarn manufacturing for over a century. This article explains how it works and why it continues to be the dominant spinning technology.
22 September 20268 min readAKR Spinning Mills
## What ring spinning is
Ring spinning is the oldest method for spinning staple fibres (fibres of defined length, like cotton) into yarn, and despite open-end (rotor) spinning, air-jet spinning, and other newer technologies, it remains the dominant method worldwide. Ring-spun yarn has a strength, evenness, and handle that other methods have not consistently matched for knitted and woven fabric applications.
## How it works
The process starts with a roving, a thick, loosely twisted strand of fibres prepared at the simplex stage. The roving feeds into the ring frame, where three things happen at once.
First, pairs of rotating rollers draft the roving out to the required fineness. The ratio between roving count and final yarn count is the draft ratio, which can be very high on modern ring frames.
Second, the drafted fibres are twisted by the ring-and-traveller mechanism. A traveller, a small C-shaped clip, rides around a ring on the spindle and inserts twist as the spindle rotates. That twist locks the fibres together and gives the yarn its strength.
Third, as the yarn is twisted, it winds simultaneously onto the bobbin on the spindle.
## What ring-spun yarn is like
The fibres in ring-spun yarn wrap around a central core at an angle set by the twist level. That structure gives the yarn good tenacity relative to its count, controlled count variation when the drafting system is well maintained, a round and compact cross-section, and a surface hairiness from fibre ends that contributes to fabric softness in karded yarn.
## Ring spinning vs. open-end (rotor) spinning
Rotor spinning is faster and more productive. But rotor-spun yarn has fibres wrapped at a steeper angle, with some wrapping in the opposite direction, which makes it slightly lower in strength, more irregular in appearance, and better suited to coarser counts, denim, and coarse knitwear. For knitwear, jerseys, shirts, and towels, ring spinning gives a better result.
## Why we use ring spinning
We produce karded yarn for knitting and weaving where strength, roundness, and handle matter. Ring spinning delivers those properties consistently across lots, which is why it is the core of our production operation.
