### Why Karded Yarn?
Karded yarn is produced without the combing step — retaining a mix of short and long fibres. This gives the yarn a natural, slightly softer handle and makes it cost-effective for a wide range of everyday textile applications.

How We Make It
## The Spinning Process
01
STEP 01
### Opening & Cleaning
Raw cotton is opened and cleaned to remove impurities and short fibres.
02
STEP 02
### Carding
Fibres are aligned into a continuous sliver; short fibres and neps are removed.
03
STEP 03
### Drawing
Multiple card slivers are combined and drawn to improve evenness.
04
STEP 04
### Roving
The drawn sliver is attenuated further and given a slight twist to prepare for spinning.
05
STEP 05
### Spinning
Roving is drafted and twisted into yarn on ring spinning frames.
06
STEP 06
### Winding
Yarn is wound onto cones and inspected for defects during transfer.
07
STEP 07
### Testing
Finished yarn is tested for count, strength, evenness, hairiness, and imperfections.

About ⟨/about⟩/HistoryThree-Generation Heritage
# Traders. Ginners. Spinners.
We didn't start in spinning. We started as raw cotton traders under N. Srinivasan, expanded into ginners, and grew into ring spinners under MD Asok Kumar and JMD Keerthan Asok Kumar.
First Generation · Founded by N. Srinivasan
## The Foundation: Cotton Trading
Raw Cotton Merchanting & Market Knowledge
The AKR story begins with our founder, N. Srinivasan. Operating in the fertile cotton heartlands of Tamil Nadu, he built the enterprise from the ground up as a raw cotton trader.
In the early days of cotton merchanting, business was forged on word of mouth, sharp eye for staple length, and fair dealing with local growers. N. Srinivasan developed an intimate understanding of cotton varieties, micronaire values, and seasonal yields — instilling the core values of integrity and fibre knowledge that continue to define AKR.
Second Generation · Led by Asok Kumar (MD)
## The Expansion: Ginning to Ring Spinning
Ginning Facilities & Commissioning the Spinning Mill
Building on the trading foundation, Asok Kumar expanded the business into cotton ginning. By setting up ginning operations, the family took control of the separation of lint from seed — ensuring superior fibre cleanliness and minimal contamination.
Recognizing that their profound knowledge of cotton fibre gave them a unique edge over conventional mill owners, Asok Kumar made the visionary leap from ginning into manufacturing, establishing AKR Spinning Mills.
He oversaw the acquisition and commissioning of high-precision ring spinning machinery from global leaders — including LMW and Trützschler blowroom and carding lines, Rieter draw frames, and Saurer and Muratec autoconers.
Third Generation · Operated by Keerthan Asok Kumar (JMD)
## Today & Beyond: Modernization & Diversification
Blended Fibres, Combed Yarns & Uster Standards
Today, AKR Spinning Mills is managed by the third generation, with Keerthan Asok Kumar serving as Joint Managing Director.
Carrying forward the family's deep-rooted respect for cotton, Keerthan Asok Kumar has spearheaded technical modernization — benchmarking production to international Uster standards and expanding the product portfolio beyond pure karded cotton to include 100% Combed Cotton, high-tenacity Polyester Cotton (PC), and breathable Cotton Polyester (CP / CVC) blends.
Under this three-generation continuity, AKR maintains its defining distinction: buyers communicate directly with the family leadership, ensuring personal accountability for every cone shipped.

# Seven Stages.
One Consistent Yarn.
Every cone of AKR karded yarn passes through a structured, seven-stage ring spinning process. Understanding how we make it helps you understand why you can rely on it.
01Opening & Cleaning ⟨#stage-1⟩02Carding ⟨#stage-2⟩03Drawing & Doubling ⟨#stage-3⟩04Roving / Simplex ⟨#stage-4⟩05Ring Spinning ⟨#stage-5⟩06Autoconer Winding ⟨#stage-6⟩07Testing & Quality Release ⟨#stage-7⟩
STAGE 01
## Opening & Cleaning
LMW & Trützschler Full Line
Remove impurities and open raw cotton and blend fibres
Raw cotton bales and synthetic fibres are opened on our automated Trützschler and LMW blowroom lines. A synchronized sequence of coarse and fine openers, dust extractors, and micro-cleaners separates the fibres gently while removing leaf particles, seed coats, and micro-dust without fibre degradation.
Quality Checkpoint
Trash content and fibre tuft regularity monitored continuously at blowroom exit.
STAGE 02
## Carding
Trützschler & LMW High-Production Cards
Individualize fibres and extract remaining neps and short fibres
Treated fibres pass through high-production Trützschler and LMW carding frames. Precision revolving wire flats individualize tangled clumps into singular parallel fibres, systematically removing neps and short fly waste to deliver a clean, uniform sliver.
Quality Checkpoint
Sliver weight (hank), nep count, and short fibre percentage tested at card delivery.
STAGE 03
## Drawing & Doubling
LMW & Rieter Autoleveller Draw Frames
Improve sliver evenness, parallelize fibres, and blend components
Multiple card slivers are combined and drafted on Rieter and LMW autoleveller draw frames. Digital autolevelling compensates for sliver mass variations instantaneously, ensuring perfect fibre parallelism and uniform blend distribution for 100% cotton, PC, and CP yarns.
Quality Checkpoint
Sliver evenness (CV% < 0.6%) and linear density verified with continuous autoleveller monitoring.
STAGE 04
## Roving / Simplex
LMW Speed Frames
Attenuate sliver and apply protective twist for ring spinning
The drawn sliver is drafted down on LMW speed frames and given a controlled protective twist to create a slender roving. Roving bobbins are loaded onto overhead creel tracks to eliminate manual handling shocks before ring drafting.
Quality Checkpoint
Roving count (hank), twist factor, and bobbin tension uniformity verified.
STAGE 05
## Ring Spinning
LMW Ring Spinning Fleet
Draft, insert final twist, and spin into finished yarn
Precision LMW ring frames draft the roving to the exact yarn count (Ne 16s to Ne 60s) and insert the final twist through synchronized ring-and-traveller mechanisms. Spindle speeds and drafting zones are tailored for each count to ensure optimal tensile tenacity and low hairiness.
Quality Checkpoint
Count (Ne), twist-per-inch (TPI), breaking tenacity (cN/tex), and elongation tested regularly on spindle doffs.
STAGE 06
## Autoconer Winding
Saurer & Muratec Precision Autoconers
Transfer to export cones, clear defects, and thermo-splice
Yarn is wound from spinning bobbins onto delivery cones on Saurer and Muratec automatic winding machines. High-speed electronic yarn clearers scan every centimetre, cutting out thick spots, thin places, and neps, replacing them with knot-free splices.
Quality Checkpoint
Electronic clearer settings verified; splice strength tested; cone density checked for high-speed knitting and warping.
STAGE 07
## Testing & Quality Release
Uster Benchmarked Testing Laboratory
Verify lot-to-lot parameters meet global standards before dispatch
Yarn packages from each production lot are conditioned and tested in our laboratory against Uster global benchmark standards. Measurements cover count variation (CV%), tensile strength, elongation, mass evenness, imperfection indices, and hairiness before release.
Quality Checkpoint
Full Uster testing, tenacity testing, and comprehensive lot test certificates generated for buyer inspection.

## Understanding the Four Choices
### Polyester Cotton (PC) vs. Cotton Polyester (CP)
The difference lies in which fibre dominates the blend:
- •PC (Poly-Rich, 65/35): Maximizes tensile strength, wrinkle recovery, and longevity. Preferred for institutional uniforms, heavy workwear, and fabrics requiring high abrasion resistance.
- •CP / CVC (Cotton-Rich, 60/40): Prioritizes natural breathability, moisture management, and skin-friendly softness, with polyester providing stability and low shrinkage. Preferred for casual t-shirts, polos, and bedding.
### Karded Cotton vs. Combed Cotton
The difference lies in the mechanical combing stage:
- •Karded Yarn: Retains a natural mix of short and long fibres after carding. Bulkier hand, natural texture, highly absorbent, and cost-effective — ideal for everyday knits, fleece, and terry goods.
- •Combed Yarn: Short fibres and impurities are mechanically combed out (removing up to 18% noil). Results in an ultra-smooth, lustrous, high-strength yarn with minimal hairiness — ideal for luxury shirting, fine knits, and export-grade apparel.

Why Choose Karded Yarn
## The Benefits of Karded Cotton
### Natural Handle
Karded yarn retains a broader mix of fibre lengths than combed, giving it a slightly bulkier, softer feel — preferred for comfortable everyday garments and towels.
### Cost Efficiency
No combing step means less fibre waste (no noil removal) and a shorter process path — making karded yarn the economical choice for applications that don't require the premium finish of combing.
### Reliable Consistency
Even without combing, our carding and drawing stages are carefully managed to deliver a consistent count, controlled evenness, and minimal imperfection levels — lot after lot.
### Versatile Application
Karded yarn performs across a wide range of end products — from knitted garments and jerseys to towels and shirting fabrics — making it a versatile choice for manufacturers with varied production needs.
Natural Characteristics
## What Makes Karded Yarn What It Is
Karded yarn's character is shaped by the carding process itself — the stage that gives this yarn its name. Unlike combed yarn, where short fibres are removed by a combing cylinder, karded yarn retains a distribution of fibre lengths. This is not a flaw — it is a defining characteristic that makes karded yarn what it is.
In fabric, karded yarn produces a slightly softer, more textured surface than combed. For T-shirts, jerseys, and towels — where comfort and absorbency matter more than surface smoothness — these characteristics are an asset.
The slight hairiness of karded yarn also contributes to the softness that end-consumers expect in everyday cotton garments and household textiles.

Cost Efficiency
## Value Without Compromise
### No Combing Waste
Combing removes 12–20% of fibre as noil. Karded yarn uses the full card sliver — reducing raw material waste and keeping the process cost down.
### Shorter Process Path
Karded yarn skips the combing stage entirely. Fewer machines, fewer handling stages, and faster throughput all contribute to a leaner cost structure.
### Right Fit for Application
For everyday knitwear, jerseys, towels, and standard shirting, karded yarn delivers the required properties at a cost point that works commercially.

### Understanding Yarn Count (Ne)
Yarn count in the English (Ne) system indicates how many 840-yard hanks of yarn weigh one pound. A higher Ne number means a finer yarn — a 40s yarn is finer than a 20s yarn.
For karded yarn, the practical count range is typically in the coarser to medium segment of the market. Fine karded yarn is possible, but the natural variation from retained short fibres makes combed more suitable at very fine counts. We recommend discussing your count requirement with us directly.

# What Is Karded Yarn? A Plain-Language Guide for Textile Buyers
Karded yarn is one of the most widely used types of cotton yarn — but what exactly does 'karded' mean, and how does it differ from combed? A clear explanation for buyers.
1 September 20266 min readAKR Spinning Mills
## What carding does
Carding is a stage in the spinning process where raw cotton fibres pass through a machine fitted with fine wire-covered rollers. Those rollers separate the fibres, remove dust, small seed fragments, and short fibres, break up neps (small knots of entangled fibre), and align the surviving fibres into a continuous strand called a sliver. The sliver is then drawn and spun into yarn.
The fibres that come out of carding are a mix of lengths. That mixed length distribution is what defines karded yarn.
## How karded yarn differs from combed yarn
In karded yarn production, the card sliver goes directly to drawing and spinning. The short and long fibres stay in the yarn together.
In combed yarn production, there is one additional step after carding: the sliver passes through a combing machine that pulls out the short fibres, called noil. What remains is a sliver made almost entirely of long, parallel fibres.
That extra step changes the finished yarn. Karded yarn has a broader fibre length distribution, slightly higher hairiness, and a softer, more textured handle. Combed yarn has a longer average fibre length, lower hairiness, higher strength at equivalent count, and a smoother, more lustrous surface.
## When karded yarn is the right choice
For everyday T-shirts, jerseys, and towels, karded yarn's texture and softness suit the product well, and combing adds cost without a visible benefit. Combing removes 12-20% of the fibre as noil waste, which raises the raw material cost. Towels in particular benefit from karded cotton's higher hairiness, as the fibre ends improve moisture absorption and give the pile a soft feel.
## On quality
Karded does not mean low quality. A well-produced karded yarn with controlled count variation, low imperfections, and verified strength is a good product for the applications it is designed for. At AKR, we test against Uster benchmark standards to keep consistency across lots. The question is whether karded is the right yarn for a given application, not whether it is better or worse in general.

# Karded vs. Combed Yarn: Which Is Right for Your Product?
Choosing between karded and combed yarn depends on your end product, your quality standards, and your cost structure. This article helps you decide.
15 September 20267 min readAKR Spinning Mills
## The difference
Karded and combed yarn follow the same process through the blow room, carding, drawing, roving, ring spinning, and winding, with one exception: combed yarn goes through a combing machine between carding and drawing. That machine removes the short fibres, called noil, and leaves a sliver made primarily of longer, more parallel fibres.
## How that shows up in the yarn
Karded yarn retains a mix of short and long fibres. It has slightly higher hairiness, which gives the fabric a softer feel, and a more textured surface. It is also lower cost because there is no noil waste and the process is shorter.
Combed yarn has short fibres removed, which gives it a smoother and more lustrous surface, lower hairiness, higher strength at equivalent count, and better evenness with fewer imperfections. The noil waste (12-20% of the fibre) adds to raw material cost.
## Which to use
Karded yarn suits everyday knitted garments (T-shirts, jerseys, casual knitwear), towels and terry goods where absorbency and softness matter, and any fabric in the mainstream segment where the combed premium is not justified by the end product.
Combed yarn suits premium or export fabrics where surface smoothness is critical, applications that need higher strength at fine counts, shirting fabrics with strict evenness requirements, and wherever buyer or brand specifications call for it.
A practical test: if you washed and wore a T-shirt made from our karded yarn, would you notice it wasn't combed? For most everyday garments, the answer is no. For a fine, printed shirting fabric, the evenness and lower imperfection count of combed yarn starts to matter.
## Our position
We produce karded yarn. If your product calls for combed, we would rather say so clearly than supply a yarn that isn't right for it.

# Ring Spinning Explained: Why It Remains the Most Trusted Yarn Manufacturing Method
Ring spinning has been the backbone of cotton yarn manufacturing for over a century. This article explains how it works and why it continues to be the dominant spinning technology.
22 September 20268 min readAKR Spinning Mills
## What ring spinning is
Ring spinning is the oldest method for spinning staple fibres (fibres of defined length, like cotton) into yarn, and despite open-end (rotor) spinning, air-jet spinning, and other newer technologies, it remains the dominant method worldwide. Ring-spun yarn has a strength, evenness, and handle that other methods have not consistently matched for knitted and woven fabric applications.
## How it works
The process starts with a roving, a thick, loosely twisted strand of fibres prepared at the simplex stage. The roving feeds into the ring frame, where three things happen at once.
First, pairs of rotating rollers draft the roving out to the required fineness. The ratio between roving count and final yarn count is the draft ratio, which can be very high on modern ring frames.
Second, the drafted fibres are twisted by the ring-and-traveller mechanism. A traveller, a small C-shaped clip, rides around a ring on the spindle and inserts twist as the spindle rotates. That twist locks the fibres together and gives the yarn its strength.
Third, as the yarn is twisted, it winds simultaneously onto the bobbin on the spindle.
## What ring-spun yarn is like
The fibres in ring-spun yarn wrap around a central core at an angle set by the twist level. That structure gives the yarn good tenacity relative to its count, controlled count variation when the drafting system is well maintained, a round and compact cross-section, and a surface hairiness from fibre ends that contributes to fabric softness in karded yarn.
## Ring spinning vs. open-end (rotor) spinning
Rotor spinning is faster and more productive. But rotor-spun yarn has fibres wrapped at a steeper angle, with some wrapping in the opposite direction, which makes it slightly lower in strength, more irregular in appearance, and better suited to coarser counts, denim, and coarse knitwear. For knitwear, jerseys, shirts, and towels, ring spinning gives a better result.
## Why we use ring spinning
We produce karded yarn for knitting and weaving where strength, roundness, and handle matter. Ring spinning delivers those properties consistently across lots, which is why it is the core of our production operation.

# Understanding Yarn Count: English Ne Explained
Yarn count tells you how fine or coarse a yarn is — but the numbering system can be confusing. This guide explains English count (Ne) in plain language for garment and fabric buyers.
8 September 20265 min readAKR Spinning Mills
## What yarn count means
Yarn count tells you how thick or thin a yarn is. A higher count number means a finer yarn; a lower count number means a coarser yarn. That seems backwards at first, but it follows from how the count is measured.
## The English count system (Ne)
Ne is the count system used for cotton yarn in India and in most global cotton trade. It is sometimes written simply as "s" (30s, 40s). The definition: how many 840-yard hanks of that yarn weigh one pound.
A 30s yarn means 30 hanks of 840 yards (25,200 yards total) weigh 1 pound. A 40s yarn means 40 hanks of 840 yards (33,600 yards) weigh the same pound. Since the 40 hanks weigh the same as the 30 hanks, each 40s hank is lighter, which means the yarn is finer.
## Count ranges and typical applications
As a rough guide for karded cotton yarn:
- Below 20s: heavy fabrics, canvas, rope, thick jersey, blankets
- 20s to 40s: everyday knitwear, T-shirts, jerseys, towels, casual shirting
- Above 40s: finer knitwear, lighter fabrics, better shirting
For everyday T-shirts and jerseys, the 20s to 30s range is most common. Towels use coarser counts because the thick pile structure needs good fibre cover.
## Count variation (CV%)
CV% measures how consistent the yarn count is along its length and across packages. A lower CV% means more consistent yarn, which gives you more consistent fabric weight and appearance. When enquiring with us, specifying your target count and acceptable CV% helps us confirm whether our current production fits.
## Tex and denier
Tex (grams per 1,000 metres) and denier (grams per 9,000 metres) are used for other fibre types. For cotton yarn sourcing, Ne is the system to use. If you have a count in another system and need a conversion, send us a message.

# Warping vs. Weft Yarn: Standards for High-Speed Airjet & Sulzer Looms
High-speed airjet and Sulzer looms demand strict yarn mechanical benchmarks. Discover how AKR tests 100% karded cotton and CP yarns in-house on Uster Tester 5 (UT5) post-winding to ensure peak weaving efficiency.
29 September 20266 min readAKR Spinning Mills
## Warp and weft yarn: what the difference means for high-speed weaving
Warp and weft yarns sit in the same fabric but do completely different jobs. Warp yarns run lengthwise under continuous tension through shedding, sizing, and reed abrasion. Weft yarns are inserted across the shed at very high velocity, then beaten in. Each environment asks for different things from the yarn.
## What high-speed looms need
Airjet looms run weft insertion at 1,000 to 1,200 metres per minute. At that speed, any surface hairiness on the yarn causes warp clinging or missed weft arrivals, which stops the loom. Sulzer projectile and high-speed rapier looms apply intense cyclic tensile loading during shedding and beat-up. The yarn needs high Count Strength Product (CSP), consistent twist, and good elongation recovery to handle the reciprocating forces without breaking.
## Warp yarn requirements
The warp sheet runs under continuous tension from beam to fell. During that journey it passes through reed dents, heald eyes, and sizing baths, all of which abrade the yarn surface.
- High breaking tenacity to survive peak beat-up tension without end-breaks
- Low elongation CV% to keep tension even across the width and avoid beam barrel distortion or warp stripiness
- Minimal thin places (-50%), since thin places break first under shed opening tension
- Low hairiness index (H), because protruding fibres interlock between adjacent warp ends during shedding and cause entrapment
- Knot-free pneumatic splices at 80-85% of parent yarn strength minimum
## Weft yarn requirements
Weft yarn faces a different problem: extreme instantaneous acceleration across the reed, then a stop.
- Uniform winding density and precise angle of wind on the cone, with zero slough-offs during high-velocity takeoff
- Consistent twist along the length to prevent snarling or loop formation inside the airjet profile reed
- Low friction coefficient so the yarn travels cleanly without needing excessive pneumatic pressure
- Tight lot-to-lot count consistency, since any variation shows up immediately as horizontal weft bars in the cloth
## How AKR tests against these requirements
Every production lot is tested post-winding directly from the autoconer packages, not from intermediate stages. We test on an in-house Uster Tester 5 (UT5) and document:
- U% and CVm% for mass evenness
- Thin places (-50%), thick places (+50%), and neps (+200%) per 1,000 metres
- Hairiness H value against benchmark limits
- Tensile tenacity and elongation on automated strength testers
Test data and sample cones are available before any commercial commitment.

Textile manufacturing requires ongoing technical knowledge. We invest in training our production staff — from machine operators to quality technicians — to ensure they can work confidently with the equipment and processes we run.
### Trainee Programmes
We create entry pathways for young people entering the textile industry. Trainees work alongside experienced production staff, building practical skills in spinning, winding, and quality control that can support a long career in the sector.
### Employee Benefits & Support
We support our workforce with fair wages, safe working conditions, and where possible, access to basic welfare provisions. The wellbeing of the people who come to work for AKR every day is a responsibility we take seriously.
### Workplace Development
A respectful, safe, and supportive workplace is not a nice-to-have — it is the foundation on which a reliable production team is built. We work to maintain an environment where people can do their best work.
### People Development
We recognise that skilled textile workers are not just employees — they are custodians of industry knowledge. Supporting their professional development is good for them, for AKR, and for the broader textile ecosystem in Tamil Nadu.
### Community Initiatives
As a family business rooted in Tamil Nadu, we have a stake in the communities around us. Our community involvement is guided by what is genuine and useful — rather than by what looks good on paper.

What we can tell you is that supporting our employees, creating entry pathways for trainees, and contributing to the communities around us are genuine priorities — not marketing exercises.
As our programmes become more formalised, we will update this page with accurate, verified information. We believe that less said accurately is better than more said loosely.
